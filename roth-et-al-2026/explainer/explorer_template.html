How the calculation works

The unit. A base neuron fires with a hard refractory period (inter-spike intervals are the refractory period plus an exponential, rate-corrected so the firing rate comes out as set). A contaminating source fires as a Poisson process with no refractory period. Contamination is the fraction of all spikes that come from the contaminating source. This is the model used throughout the manuscript.

Sliding RP. For each candidate window τr (every autocorrelogram bin, 1/30,000 s, up to 10 ms), count the observed violations Vo: spike pairs closer than τr. If the unit were contaminated at exactly the threshold C, the expected count is Ve = 2τrNc(Nb + (Nc − 1)/2)/D (Llobet et al.), with Nc contaminating and Nb base spikes in a recording of duration D. The confidence is the probability of seeing more than Vo violations from a Poisson count with mean Ve. The unit is accepted if the confidence reaches the confidence threshold at any window longer than τmin. No refractory period has to be assumed. Cmin is the lowest contamination threshold that would be accepted.

Hill–Llobet. Count violations inside one fixed, assumed refractory period and convert the count to a point estimate of contamination, 1 − √(1 − VoD / (τN2)). The unit is accepted if the estimate is at or below the threshold (equivalently, if Vo ≤ Ve). There is no statistical test, so a low-rate unit with no violations is always accepted, and a clean unit whose true refractory period is shorter than the assumed one is rejected.

What this page leaves out. Sliding RP takes the best of many windows without a multiple-comparisons correction, so for a unit sitting exactly at the threshold it accepts noticeably more often than the confidence threshold implies; try it with the 100-train button. The simulation has no bursting, drift, or correlation between the neuron and its contamination.

Where the numbers come from. The calculations reproduce the slidingRP Python package: autocorrelograms exactly, confidences to within 10−9, and acceptance rates of the simulator within sampling error of the package's own simulator.
